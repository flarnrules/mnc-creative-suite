07 / How the shader works

The card is a rounded 3D mesh with your printed artwork mapped onto its front. The fragment shader runs once per visible pixel.

Color: card coordinates, light angle, time and your rotation feed a rainbow function. Rotation contributes strength × (sin(turn) + 0.5 × sin(tilt)) degrees.

Pattern: bands, cells, stars or a bounded fractal calculation vary the foil strength and hue across the surface.

Coverage: an artwork rectangle selects art-only foil; its inverse selects reverse foil. A separate lettering mask protects the text.

Shine: the surface normal, light direction and viewing direction create highlights. Roughness broadens them; sparkle adds sparse glints. Relief perturbs the lighting normal using image brightness—it does not deform the card mesh.

Space finishes: layered stars, a radial void, turbulent clouds, dark facets and eight-petal contours use the same coverage mask. Foil intensity controls darkening and reflection together. These are mathematical patterns; no new artwork textures are required.

These are stylized real-time effects, not a physical diffraction simulation. Fractal loops stop at 24 iterations; animation targets 30 FPS.
